#### Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** High specificity. The page is authored around a checklist operation rather than a generic SaaS promise: queue, operator, manager, exception, evidence, and the three product tabs all reinforce one mental model. HAWKS BI now reads as the factual maker and trust layer, using the confirmed language of data, automation, and technology.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json src/App.tsx` returned `[]`; no bundled detector findings were available to merge or ignore.

**Visual evidence:** Existing desktop, mobile, and full-page captures were inspected as incumbent evidence. A live browser backend was unavailable, so no fresh browser render, overlay, or console evidence could be claimed.

#### Overall Impression

This is a confident, product-led landing page with a clear operational story. The single biggest opportunity is to replace the mailto fallback with a real, measurable demonstration flow and verified product proof before launch.

#### What’s Working

- The hero shows the product’s mechanism immediately instead of asking visitors to infer it from abstract copy.
- The repeated sequence “configure → execute → act by exception” gives the page a memorable spine.
- HAWKS BI’s official language, contact domain, and institutional route are now visible without stealing Smile Check’s hierarchy.

#### Priority Issues

- **[P1] The demo CTA still falls back to mailto.** This makes conversion dependent on a local email client and prevents reliable funnel measurement. Connect `VITE_SMILE_CHECK_DEMO_URL` to a confirmed scheduling or lead-capture endpoint and instrument hero/final CTA separately. Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`.
- **[P1] Synthetic metrics can still read like outcomes.** Values such as 12 routines, 92% compliance, and 09 / 12 are clearly framed as illustrative in the interface, but a permissioned redacted product capture would create stronger commercial proof. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.
- **[P2] The interactive scene remains a visual demo, not a complete product interaction.** Tabs are keyboard-focusable and expose their controlled panel, but the mock filter/sidebar are intentionally non-functional. Keep them semantically decorative or replace them with a real product preview. Suggested command: `$impeccable polish`.
- **[P2] The page is visually rich enough that another full section would add noise.** If future work expands the page, distill repeated eyebrow/card patterns before adding more proof. Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`.

#### Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan (First-Timer):** Understands “checklist operacional” quickly, but cannot yet tell whether Smile Check is aimed at food service, manufacturing, or another multi-sector context because the page stays intentionally industry-neutral.
- **Alex (Power User / buyer):** Can inspect the three product states, but the real next step is a mail client rather than a trackable workflow, so intent and follow-up are hard to verify.
- **Marina (Operations Manager):** Sees a credible exception/evidence model, but the illustrative numbers do not answer whether the system has delivered results in a real operation.

#### Minor Observations

- The external HAWKS BI actions warn assistive technology that they open a new tab, and the destination is confirmed as `https://hawksbi.com.br`.
- The focus-visible outline and reduced-motion fallback now cover the new CTA and the existing hero motion.
- The in-page scene filter is explicitly decorative because it has no behavior; this avoids presenting a dead control as functional UI.

#### Questions to Consider

- What real scheduling or lead-capture endpoint should replace the mailto fallback?
- Which permissioned product sequence can replace the synthetic dashboard values?
- Should the industry-neutral positioning stay broad, or should the first screen name the strongest vertical?
